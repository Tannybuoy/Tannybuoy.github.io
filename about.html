How I Approach Product My Actual Operating System

01

Understand the User Deeply Actually Talk to Humans

Every product decision starts with empathy. I prioritize direct user research — interviews, observations, data analysis — to build products that solve real problems, not assumed ones.

I talk to actual humans before writing a PRD. Revolutionary, I know. You'd be amazed how many PMs skip this step and then wonder why nobody uses their feature.

02

Identify the Real Constraint Find the Actual Problem

The obvious blocker is rarely the actual one. I dig into organizational, technical, and market constraints to find what's truly holding a product back — then design around it.

The stated problem is never the real problem. "We need a dashboard" actually means "my VP wants to look smart in board meetings." Finding the actual constraint saves months.

03

Design for Systems, Not Features Stop Building Frankenproducts

Features ship. Systems scale. I think about how each decision fits into the broader product ecosystem, user workflows, and business model — not just the immediate ask.

Anyone can ship a feature. The hard part is not creating a Frankenstein product in the process. I think about how things connect, or you end up with 47 settings pages.

04

Measure What Matters Vanity Metrics Are a Trap

Metrics should drive decisions, not vanity. I focus on identifying the right KPIs that connect user behavior to business outcomes, and build feedback loops that actually inform iteration.

DAU means nothing if your users are rage-clicking. I care about metrics that actually tell you if people's lives got better, not just if they opened the app.

Things I've Changed My Mind About Opinions I've Retired

Growth means updating your mental models. Here are some I've revised. Past me was wrong about a lot. Present me is probably wrong about different things.

I used to think Old me believed

"The best product always wins."

"If we build it well, they will come."

→
Now I believe Current me knows

"Distribution and timing matter as much as product quality. A good product with great GTM beats a great product with no GTM."

"Distribution eats product for breakfast. You can build the best thing ever and lose to a worse product with better sales."

I used to think Old me believed

"Data should drive every decision."

"Just show me the numbers and I'll tell you the answer."

→
Now I believe Current me knows

"Data informs decisions; judgment drives them. The most important product calls often happen in the gray area where data is ambiguous."

"Data is a flashlight, not a GPS. Sometimes you have to make the call with 60% confidence and course-correct later."

I used to think Old me believed

"More features means more value for users."

"Ship everything. Let the users sort it out."

→
Now I believe Current me knows

"The best product is the one that does less, better. Every feature you add is a feature you have to maintain, document, and support."

"Every feature is a promise you're making to support forever. The real skill is knowing what NOT to build."

I used to think Old me believed

"Consensus is the goal of every meeting."

"If everyone agrees, we've made a good decision."

→
Now I believe Current me knows

"Alignment on the problem matters more than agreement on the solution. Disagree-and-commit is a superpower."

"If everyone in the room agrees, someone isn't thinking hard enough. The best decisions come from productive disagreement."

Product Perspectives Hot Takes

"The best PRD is the one your team actually reads. Format matters less than clarity."

"Nobody reads your PRD. Write it anyway. It's for you, not them."

"A roadmap is a communication tool, not a commitment. Treat it as a living document."

"Your roadmap is fiction. Useful fiction, but fiction."

"Five user interviews reveal more than five thousand survey responses."

"If you haven't talked to a user this month, you're guessing. And you're probably wrong."

"Managing up is a product skill, not a political one. It's about creating shared context."

"Half of product management is therapy for executives. The other half is translating English to English."

"AI augments product managers. It doesn't replace the need for judgment and user empathy."

"If AI can do your PM job, you were just a ticket-writer anyway."

"North star metrics only work if the whole organization navigates by them."

"Every team has a north star metric. Most of them are wrong."
